# MovesMethod – Context & Decision Framework

## Purpose

MovesMethod is functional strength training used as **structural infrastructure** to support long-term endurance cycling, connective tissue resilience, and athletic longevity.

It is not treated as primary training stress and must not compete with cycling volume or intensity. Its role is to preserve mobility, joint integrity, balance, and movement quality as cycling load accumulates over years.

---

## Role in Training System

MovesMethod serves four functions:

1. **Connective tissue adaptation** (tendons, ligaments, fascia)
2. **End-range control and tolerance**
3. **Balance and unilateral stability**
4. **Injury risk reduction from repetitive cycling load**

MovesMethod is compatible with Zone 2 cycling and rest days, but should be limited or skipped on high-intensity cycling days.

---

## What MovesMethod Is Not

- **Not metabolic conditioning** – Heart rate elevation is incidental, not a training target
- **Not strength testing** – Load chasing and PR attempts defeat the purpose
- **Not soreness-driven** – Sessions that meaningfully interfere with next-day cycling are mis-scaled
- **Not heart-rate–based training** – Breathing is the primary load signal

Any session that compromises the ability to perform endurance training the following day (unless explicitly planned) indicates excessive load.

---

## RPE Interpretation Framework

### Dual-Domain RPE

RPE is interpreted differently in MovesMethod than in endurance sports.

- **Systemic RPE** reflects whole-body load
- **Local RPE** reflects balance, coordination, isometric tension, or joint-specific challenge

Local RPE may be high while systemic RPE remains low.

### Systemic RPE Anchors

Systemic RPE is anchored to **breathing and recovery**, not heart-rate zones.

| Indicator | Systemic RPE |
| --- | --- |
| Calm nasal breathing during sets | ≤3 |
| Brief mouth breathing between sets | ~4 |
| Mouth breathing during sets | >5 (excessive for MovesMethod) |

Transient heart-rate elevation (including brief Zone 2 HR) does not indicate aerobic or metabolic load in MovesMethod.

---

## Breathing Rules (Primary Signal)

- **Nasal breathing during sets** → acceptable systemic load
- **Brief mouth breathing between sets** → upper acceptable bound
- **Mouth breathing during sets** → excessive systemic load

Breathing takes priority over heart rate for load classification.

---

## Failure Taxonomy

Not all failure is equal. MovesMethod distinguishes three types:

### 1. Technical Failure (Acceptable)

- Loss of balance
- Form breakdown
- Inability to maintain quality
- Coordination failure

This is the preferred stopping point.

### 2. Local Muscular Failure (Occasional)

- Severe local fatigue or shaking
- Completion possible only with degraded form

Acceptable occasionally but not the target.

### 3. Systemic Failure (Not Acceptable)

- Sustained mouth breathing during sets
- Global exhaustion
- Poor recovery between sets

This indicates excessive load.

**Rule:** Never chase failure. Stop at the first clear loss of quality.

---

## Volume & Scaling Rules

When load is too high, adjustments must follow this order:

1. Shorten sets or holds
2. Increase rest time
3. Reduce number of sets
4. Regress movement difficulty (last resort)

Movement quality is preserved before progression.

---

## Progression Logic

MovesMethod progression is **phase-based, not calendar-based**.

- Weeks represent conceptual phases
- Repeating a workout or week is expected
- Progression requires repeatable quality without accumulating pain or fatigue

Completion of prescribed volume is less important than consistency and tolerance.

---

## Re-Entry Rule (Critical)

Time away from MovesMethod requires **re-entry scaling**, regardless of what phase you were in previously.

- First session after a gap is treated as reacquaintance, not continuation
- Volume is reduced (typically 50–60% of previous tolerance)
- Emphasis is on pattern exposure and movement quality, not adaptation
- Progression resumes only after tolerance is re-established across 2–3 sessions

**Key principle:** Phase labels describe training intent, not current tissue readiness. After time away, tissue readiness resets even if the program phase doesn't.

Examples of gaps requiring re-entry:

- 7+ days away from MovesMethod
- Illness or injury
- Travel or life disruption
- Medical procedures affecting movement capacity

---

## Cycling Integration Rules

- Compatible with Zone 2 endurance rides
- Preferred on rest or recovery days
- Avoid stacking full MovesMethod sessions with hard cycling intensity
- Unilateral + overhead sessions (e.g., Week 3 Workout B–type stressors) are treated as primary stressors

**Target feeling post-session:** "Worked locally, intact globally."

On endurance-heavy days, MovesMethod should be shortened or omitted entirely rather than forced to completion.

---

## Logging Guidance

- Logged RPE reflects **systemic load**, not local difficulty
- Typical systemic RPE range: **4–5**
- Notes should capture:
  - Technical failure points
  - Asymmetries
  - Local difficulty
- RPE corrections (e.g., adjusting down after reflection) are valid and encouraged

---

## Success Criteria

A successful MovesMethod session:

- **Preserves next-day readiness** – You can execute planned cycling without compromise
- **Produces no joint pain** – Discomfort and local fatigue are acceptable; pain is not
- **Ends with confidence in repeatability** – You could do this again in 2–3 days
- **Feels deliberately under-reached** – Not maximized or exhausted

Long-term consistency outweighs short-term completeness.

---

## Governing Principles

- MovesMethod is infrastructure, not competition
- Discomfort is acceptable; pain is not
- Quality over completion
- Longevity over short-term performance
- Structural consistency beats intensity

The objective is to enable continued cycling performance across decades.
