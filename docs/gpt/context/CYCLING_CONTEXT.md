## Role of Cycling

Cycling is the primary training modality and the dominant source of training load.

All non-cycling activities (strength, mobility, recovery work) exist to:

- support cycling performance
- reduce injury risk
- preserve long-term riding capacity

When conflicts arise, cycling sustainability takes precedence over auxiliary training.

---

## Primary Cycling Objectives

1. **Maintain the ability to ride frequently and consistently**
2. **Preserve aerobic base and metabolic efficiency**
3. **Avoid overuse injuries and connective tissue breakdown**
4. **Support gradual, sustainable performance improvements**
5. **Preserve enjoyment and psychological durability**

Peak fitness is secondary to durability and repeatability.

---

## Endurance Model Assumptions

- Zone 2 endurance riding is foundational and protected.
- Aerobic adaptations are prioritized over anaerobic capacity.
- Fitness gains are expected to accrue slowly and persistently.
- Short-term intensity is used sparingly and intentionally.

Loss of consistency is considered a greater failure than temporary under-reaching.

---

## Intensity & Load Interpretation

### Heart Rate

- Heart rate zones are primary indicators during cycling.
- Time-in-zone is meaningful for endurance sessions.
- Drift and decoupling matter more than single-session peaks.

### RPE

- Cycling RPE correlates reasonably with heart rate and metabolic load.
- Sustained mouth breathing during cycling indicates moderate-to-high systemic stress.
- RPE must be interpreted in context of duration and cumulative load.

---

## Fatigue & Recovery Signals

Key indicators of excessive cycling load include:

- Inability to complete planned endurance rides
- Elevated perceived effort at normally easy intensities
- Poor recovery between sessions
- Rising discomfort or pain in knees, hips, back, or feet

Short-term fatigue is acceptable; accumulating dysfunction is not.

---

## Integration with Non-Cycling Training

- Zone 2 cycling pairs well with mobility and structural work.
- High-intensity cycling should not be stacked with high structural or unilateral training.
- On days of significant cycling stress, non-cycling work should be reduced or omitted.
- Recovery days may include light mobility or structural maintenance but no competing load.

Non-cycling training must never compromise the ability to ride the following day unless intentionally planned.

---

## Progression Philosophy

- Cycling progression is trend-based, not session-based.
- Temporary plateaus are acceptable and expected.
- Regression is preferable to injury or forced time off.

Progress is defined as:
> “Still riding well next month.”

---

## Risk Management Principles

- Pain is a stop signal; discomfort is contextual.
- Minor niggles should trigger load adjustment, not denial.
- Repeated warning signs require action even if metrics look acceptable.
- Long layoffs are considered a failure of judgment, not toughness.

---

## Logging & Interpretation Preferences

- Cycling sessions should be interpreted using duration, intensity distribution, and recovery impact.
- Single hard days do not justify conclusions without context.
- Longitudinal patterns matter more than best efforts.

Corrections and reinterpretations of past sessions are valid and encouraged.

---

## Governing Principle

Cycling is the engine.  
Everything else exists to keep the engine running.

Consistency beats heroics.
