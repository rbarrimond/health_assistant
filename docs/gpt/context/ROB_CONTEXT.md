## Identity & Orientation

Robert is an endurance-focused cyclist whose primary athletic love is cycling, but whose training philosophy is anchored by injury resilience, structural integrity, and consistency over short-term performance peaks.

**Athlete ID**: rob

Training decisions should favor:

- durability over maximal output
- repeatability over novelty
- judgment over rigid adherence to plans
- mobility and structural balance as prerequisites for sustainable performance
- targeted upper-body development when needed to reduce disproportion and improve long-term function

The objective is to maximize healthspan: remaining athletic, pain-free, structurally balanced, and capable across decades, not to maximize any single metric in isolation.

---

## Training Priorities (Ranked)

1. **Never being laid up by preventable back injury, nerve irritation, or structural dysfunction**
2. **Ability to ride consistently year-round**
3. **Structural balance, symmetry, mobility, movement quality, and long-term joint integrity**
4. **Durable, punchy cycling fitness that supports both endurance and repeatable hard efforts**
5. **Increased upper-body strength and muscular balance, especially where cycling leaves underdevelopment**
6. **Short-term intensity or peak fitness**

When priorities conflict, higher-ranked items always take precedence.

---

## Risk Tolerance & Stop Rules

- Discomfort is acceptable; pain is not.
- Technical failure is a valid stopping point.
- Systemic fatigue should be avoided outside of intentional cycling sessions.
- Injury prevention outweighs completion of prescribed volume.
- Training that predictably risks back flare-ups, nerve symptoms, or loss of riding consistency should be modified early, not pushed through.
- Repeating sessions or phases is preferred to forcing progression.

If evidence is ambiguous, conservative interpretation is preferred.

---

## Effort & Fatigue Philosophy

- Global fatigue is assessed via breathing, recovery, and next-day readiness.
- Local difficulty (e.g., balance, isometrics, unilateral work) may be high without indicating excessive systemic load.
- Short-term under-reaching is acceptable if it preserves long-term consistency.
- Supplemental strength and mobility work should create local adaptation without compromising the ability to ride, recover, or function normally the next day.

Training should leave the athlete feeling:
> “Worked locally, intact globally.”

---

## Progression Philosophy

- Progression is phase-based, not calendar-based.
- Advancement requires repeatable quality without accumulating pain or dysfunction.
- Structural and movement adaptations are expected to progress more slowly than aerobic fitness.

Stability and tolerance must precede load or intensity.

---

## Cycling Integration Preferences

- Cycling is primary in the short term and remains the athlete’s primary sport identity.
- Zone 2 remains foundational and should usually be protected.
- Structural work and mobility work are durability infrastructure, not optional accessory work.
- Upper-body strength work is a targeted supplement to address imbalance, improve resilience, and support long-term function.
- Structural and supplemental strength work should usually support riding, but may take priority when neglect risks pain, dysfunction, or lost consistency.
- When conflicts arise, cycling intensity is reduced before structural integrity is compromised, and supplemental strength work is reduced before cycling consistency is compromised.
- Do not stack high structural stress or aggressive upper-body fatigue carelessly with high-intensity cycling.

---

## Strength and Mobility Integration

- MovesMethod or equivalent structural work is treated as protective infrastructure against back dysfunction, nerve irritation, and avoidable breakdown.
- Supplemental upper-body work should prioritize lats, scaps, delts, biceps, triceps, and grip when imbalance is evident.
- The purpose of upper-body work is not bodybuilding for its own sake, but balance, resilience, function, and long-term athletic completeness.
- Strength work should be dosed conservatively enough to preserve cycling quality, but assertively enough to correct obvious weak links.
- When goals are in short-term tension, programming should seek workable coexistence rather than false optimization of one domain at the expense of the others.

### Conflict Resolution Heuristics

- If back discomfort, nerve symptoms, or structural instability appear, immediately prioritize MovesMethod and reduce both cycling intensity and supplemental strength load.
- If cycling performance stagnates but structural integrity is stable, adjust cycling programming before removing structural or strength work.
- If upper-body fatigue interferes with riding quality, reduce upper-body volume first rather than eliminating it entirely.
- If recovery capacity is exceeded, reduce total training density rather than abandoning one domain completely.

---

## Interpretation Preferences

- Prefer breathing, recovery speed, and qualitative notes over single metrics.
- Treat heart-rate zones as contextual signals outside of endurance work.
- Value longitudinal trends over isolated sessions.
- Corrections to logged interpretations (e.g., RPE adjustments) are valid and encouraged.

---

## Units & Representation Preferences

- Prefer standard American units (miles, feet, pounds, °F) in explanations and summaries.
- Where cycling convention dominates, use domain-standard units instead (e.g., watts, watts/kg, meters, kilometers when appropriate).
- Consistency and clarity take precedence over strict unit purity; avoid unnecessary unit switching within a single explanation.

---

## Coaching & Interaction Style

- Prefer precise, analytical explanations over motivational language.
- Explicitly state uncertainty when present.
- Avoid hype, absolutism, or prescriptive certainty without evidence.
- Account explicitly for tradeoffs when cycling performance, mobility, and strength goals are in short-term tension.
- Clarity and accuracy are valued more than reassurance.

The goal is informed judgment, not compliance.
